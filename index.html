Research Profile

I am an immunology researcher with a Master's degree from Peking University (June 2026), with hands-on experience in mouse disease models, high-dimensional flow cytometry, immune-cell isolation and sorting, and tissue immunostaining. My experimental work has focused on tumour immunity and inflammatory biology, supported by computational analysis of bulk and single-cell datasets.

My practical experience includes orthotopic and subcutaneous tumour models, a mouse model of ulcerative colitis, flow-cytometric characterisation of immune populations in murine tissues, immunofluorescence and immunohistochemistry, primary-cell culture, and recombinant Fc-fusion protein expression and purification. I am interested in extending this background to innate immune regulation, granulopoiesis, and neutrophil biology.

Project fit · Key evidence

Relevance to the MICL–Neutrophil Research Project

The project in Dr Mariano Malamud's group investigates how pattern-recognition receptors regulate neutrophil activation and inflammatory responses. Although my previous research has not directly focused on neutrophils or NET formation, I have hands-on experience in several experimental areas central to the project and am keen to apply these skills to innate immunology.

Most relevant strengths: in vivo disease models · spectral flow cytometry and FACS · tissue immunostaining · recombinant Fc-fusion protein production

1. In vivo models of inflammation and disease

Position requirement: Animal models of stress-induced granulopoiesis and bacterial infection.

My experience: Hands-on work with orthotopic and subcutaneous mouse tumour models, as well as a mouse model of ulcerative colitis. These studies involved disease-model execution and assessment of tissue and immune phenotypes.

Portfolio evidence: Pancreatic tumour model and in vivo experimental figures in “Selected Experimental Results”.

2. High-dimensional flow cytometry and cell sorting

Position requirement: Spectral flow cytometry and sorting to characterise immune populations in murine tissues.

My experience: Flow-cytometric analysis of tumour-infiltrating CD8+ T cells and MHC-II+ macrophages, immune-cell phenotyping, gating-strategy development, and FACS isolation of defined stromal populations from mouse tissues.

Portfolio evidence: CD8 and macrophage flow plots, gating strategy, and FACS sorting examples below.

3. Immunohistology and tissue imaging

Position requirement: Immunohistological staining and imaging, including multiplexed immunohistochemistry.

My experience: Immunofluorescence, immunohistochemistry and multiplexed tissue staining to assess immune-cell infiltration and tissue-level changes, alongside histological evaluation of collagen deposition.

Portfolio evidence: Immunofluorescence images and Van Gieson (VG) staining in “Selected Experimental Results”.

4. Primary cells, cell lines and recombinant proteins

Position requirement: Culture of primary cells and cell lines, and biochemical investigation of inflammatory pathways.

My experience: Primary mouse and human T-cell isolation, activation and culture; cell-line culture; and design, expression and purification of recombinant Fc-fusion proteins.

Portfolio evidence: Fc-fusion protein design and purification workflow, alongside primary-cell experimental experience.

Connection to the group's Nature 2024 study

The study “Recognition and control of neutrophil extracellular trap formation by MICL” (Malamud et al., Nature, 2024) identifies MICL/CLEC12A as an inhibitory pattern-recognition receptor that recognises NET-associated DNA and restrains neutrophil activation and further NET formation. The work combines mouse inflammation and infection models, flow-cytometric analysis of myeloid populations, neutrophil functional assays, immunofluorescence for MPO and citrullinated histone H3, and an Fc–MICL fusion protein used to test NET recognition.

My strongest points of overlap are in vivo modelling, immune-cell phenotyping by flow cytometry, tissue staining and imaging, and recombinant Fc-fusion protein production. I have not yet worked directly with neutrophil isolation, NETosis or ROS assays; I would be keen to develop these specialised techniques within the group.

Reference: Malamud et al., Nature (2024), “Recognition and control of neutrophil extracellular trap formation by MICL”.
